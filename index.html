How the AI Assistant Works

Choose a tab to explore the workflow for users, advisors, and administrators.

This guide includes proposed features. Topic-model extraction, versioning, cross-user synthesis, and the private contact Inbox require implementation and testing before they are available.

From a conversation to a clearer decision

The conversation is the discussion. The topic model is a structured interpretation that people can inspect and correct. A synthesis is a separate model derived from selected, authorized sources.

  1. 01 · DISCUSS Explore one problem within a topic.
  2. 02 · STRUCTURE Build a draft model linked to source messages.
  3. 03 · REVIEW Confirm, correct, and save a model version.
  4. 04 · COMPARE Compare authorized models and develop a separate synthesis.

What a topic model contains

  • Decision question and context.
  • Goals, constraints, and priorities.
  • Options and comparison criteria.
  • Assumptions and evidence references.
  • Information gaps and unresolved questions.
  • Review status and model versions.

People remain in control

AI suggestions begin as drafts. Users confirm their circumstances and preferences. Advisors contribute feedback within their assigned access.

Important interpretations should remain open to correction, explanation, and review.

An AI-generated statement is not automatically a fact. User-reported information is not independent verification. A preference score is not a probability of success.

User guide

Explore one problem at a time and help the model reflect your actual situation.

1

Describe your problem

Explain your situation, the decision you face, and the outcome you want.

Example: Should we launch now or run a paid pilot?

2

Clarify what matters

Answer focused questions about goals, constraints, priorities, timing, and missing context.

Confirm currencies, dates, and important definitions.

3

Explore your options

Review suggested actions and comparison criteria. Add, revise, or reject items that do not fit.

An AI proposal is not automatically your preference.

4

Check the draft model

Inspect assumptions, evidence references, and information gaps. Correct misinterpretations.

Distinguish user reports, AI suggestions, and external evidence.

5

Compare trade-offs

Examine benefits, risks, uncertainty, and assigned-advisor input when available.

Inspect criteria and assumptions—not just the final ranking.

6

Review and refine

Test scenarios, review feedback, and save an updated model version as your situation develops.

Hypothetical changes should not silently replace your current circumstances.

Before continuing

  • Use separate topics for unrelated decisions.
  • Confirm important model items.
  • Avoid passwords and unnecessary sensitive details.
  • Review sharing permissions before cross-user synthesis.

Expected outputs when implemented

  • A conversation connected to one topic.
  • A source-linked topic-model draft.
  • A user-reviewed model version.
  • Unresolved questions and possible next actions.

Advisor guide

Compare cases without erasing individual context or expanding your access.

1

Select authorized topics

Choose topics and model versions available within your assigned access.

Advisor status alone should not reveal every user.

2

Review sources and status

Inspect source messages, evidence links, user confirmations, and unresolved questions.

Interpretation review is not independent factual verification.

3

Check comparability

Align decision context, criteria, units, assessment scales, and time horizons.

Similar terminology does not establish equivalent circumstances.

4

Compare side by side

Identify shared patterns, contextual differences, conflicting assumptions, and evidence gaps.

Do not average incompatible scores or hide disagreement.

5

Create a separate synthesis

Select authorized elements and record source topics, versions, mappings, and reasons for inclusion.

Preserve original users’ models unchanged.

6

Return reviewed feedback

Explain limitations, invite corrections, and share only with the approved audience.

Derived outputs must not reveal unauthorized details.

Expected outputs when implemented

  • Topic-specific review and comments.
  • A comparability matrix.
  • A separate synthesis model.
  • Source references and review history.

Preserve differences

Keep conflicting assumptions, priorities, and circumstances visible.

A pattern across selected cases does not automatically apply to each individual.

Administrator guide

Keep identity management, participant eligibility, advisor assignments, and access to private content separate.

1

Manage identities and roles

Designate privileged accounts through a protected administrative process.

Ordinary registration must not grant administrator access.

2

Configure access scopes

Manage participant eligibility, advisor assignments, and explicit sharing permissions.

Hiding a menu item is not backend authorization.

3

Maintain context templates

Version criterion definitions, units, required questions, and assessment scales.

Template updates must not silently rewrite old models.

4

Monitor model operations

Track extraction errors, validation, source-link quality, reviews, and usage costs.

A correctly formatted AI response can still be wrong.

5

Manage contact requests

Use the planned private Inbox to review inquiries and monitor notification status.

Saving a request and delivering an email are separate outcomes.

6

Maintain and audit

Review privileged access, record changes, test recovery, and apply retention policies.

Retention and deletion must consider derived models too.

Planned contact-request workflow

  1. 01 · VALIDATE Check the public inquiry.
  2. 02 · SAVE Store the request and pending notification.
  3. 03 · INBOX Display the request to authorized administrators.
  4. 04 · NOTIFY Process the email notification separately.
Email failure must not erase the stored request. Provider acceptance does not guarantee inbox delivery. This guide does not implement the Inbox or email worker.
  • Keep privileged keys and email secrets off the browser.
  • Protect public submissions against spam and abuse.
  • Test access using separate accounts for each role.
  • Verify features before presenting them as available.

Shared context and cross-user modeling

Use a common framework while preserving individual goals, constraints, preferences, evidence, and uncertainty.

  1. 01 · SOURCES Separate topic-model versions.
  2. 02 · PERMISSIONS Check access and approved sharing scope.
  3. 03 · COMPARISON Align definitions and flag differences.
  4. 04 · SYNTHESIS Create a separate, traceable model.

Shared definitions

A context template defines a decision family, criteria, units, assessment scales, and required context questions.

Individual circumstances

Each topic retains its own assumptions, constraints, priorities, evidence, and unresolved questions.

Example: product-launch decisions

Two users may share demand evidence, readiness, cost, and learning value as criteria.

One prioritizes learning before committing; another prioritizes speed. Their recommendations can legitimately differ.

Three distinct advisor actions
Action Purpose Protection
Compare Display compatible dimensions side by side. Do not modify source models.
Reuse Propose a criterion, question, or template. Stay within authorized scope and request review.
Synthesize Create a separate model from selected elements. Retain source versions, mappings, and permissions.

Review and versioning

  1. CURRENT Existing model version.
  2. INPUT New information or a hypothetical scenario.
  3. REVIEW Proposed change and human review.
  4. NEXT New model version or separate scenario.

Permissions must apply to records, searches, exports, and derived outputs—not just navigation links.

Questions and important boundaries

Open a question to read its explanation.

Does the AI make the final decision?

No. The proposed workflow organizes options, evidence, uncertainty, and trade-offs. Users retain judgment and may seek suitable professional advice for consequential decisions.

Can advisors see every conversation?

Not under this proposed policy. Access should be restricted by assignments and explicit sharing rules. Check the implemented policy before entering confidential information.

Does similarity allow topics to be merged?

No. Similarity may identify comparison candidates. Permission and compatibility must be checked before creating a separate synthesis.

What if the model gets something wrong?

Flag the item, inspect its source, and propose a correction. Save a reviewed version rather than silently rewriting history.

What does a score of 75 out of 100 mean?

Only what the defined scoring method says. It is not a 75% chance of success. Criteria, scales, weights, and assumptions should be visible.

Does this guide connect to Supabase?

No. This component displays instructions and switches tabs. It does not sign users in, change roles, save models, or send email.

Are all described features available now?

No. Topic-model extraction, versioning, cross-user synthesis, and the private contact Inbox are planned until implemented and tested.

AI supports your judgment—it does not replace it.
