From a conversation to a clearer decision

A conversation is the discussion. A topic model is a structured interpretation that users can inspect and correct. A synthesis is a separate model derived from selected, authorized sources.

The topic model

Decision question, goals, constraints, options, criteria, assumptions, evidence references, information gaps, review status, and version history.

People remain in control

AI suggestions begin as drafts. Users confirm their circumstances and preferences. Advisors add review within their assigned scope.

A preference score is not a probability of success. User-reported information is not independently verified evidence.

Choose your guide

Users: build and refine a topic. Advisors: review, compare, and synthesize. Administrators: manage identities, access policies, and operations.

User guide

Explore one problem at a time and help the model reflect your actual situation.

1

Define your topic

Describe the decision, situation, and desired outcome. Keep unrelated decisions in separate topics.

Example: Should we launch now or run a paid pilot?

2

Clarify what matters

Answer questions about goals, constraints, priorities, time horizon, and missing context.

Confirm currencies, dates, and the meaning of important terms.

3

Explore your options

Add, revise, or reject suggested actions and comparison criteria.

An AI proposal is not automatically your preference.

4

Review the draft model

Inspect each important item and its source-message references. Correct gaps or misinterpretations.

Separate user reports, AI suggestions, assumptions, and external evidence.

5

Compare and seek review

Examine benefits, risks, and uncertainty. Request assigned-advisor input when available.

Inspect any scoring method rather than accepting an unexplained ranking.

6

Refine your next step

Test scenarios and save reviewed model versions as the situation develops.

A hypothetical budget change should not replace the actual budget.

Expected outputs when implemented

A source-linked topic model, a reviewed version, unresolved questions, and possible next actions.

Before continuing

  • Confirm important model items.
  • Do not enter passwords or unnecessary sensitive details.
  • Review the sharing scope before cross-user synthesis.
  • Ask for correction when AI output misrepresents you.

Advisor guide

Compare cases without erasing individual circumstances or expanding your access.

1

Select authorized models

Choose accessible topics and specific model versions.

An advisor role alone should not reveal every user’s records.

2

Inspect sources and status

Review conversation references, evidence links, user confirmations, and unresolved gaps.

Review status and factual verification are different things.

3

Check comparability

Align decision context, criteria definitions, units, scales, and time horizons.

Similar terminology does not establish comparable circumstances.

4

Compare side by side

Identify shared patterns while retaining conflicting assumptions and constraints.

Do not average incompatible scores or hide disagreement.

5

Create a separate synthesis

Select authorized elements and record source topic, version, item, mapping, and reason.

Do not overwrite the source users’ models.

6

Review and return feedback

Explain limitations and share only within the approved audience. Invite corrections.

Derived outputs must not disclose unauthorized source details.

Expected outputs when implemented

Topic-specific comments, a comparability matrix, and a separate reviewed synthesis with source references.

Keep disagreements visible

Record differences in priorities, assumptions, evidence, and definitions. A group finding should not automatically become an individual recommendation.

Administrator guide

Keep operations, research eligibility, and access to private content explicitly separated.

1

Manage identities and roles

Assign privileged roles through a protected administrative process.

Ordinary registration must not grant administrator access.

2

Configure access scopes

Manage participant eligibility, advisor assignments, and sharing permissions.

Hiding a navigation item is not backend authorization.

3

Maintain context templates

Version criterion definitions, units, required questions, and assessment scales.

Template updates must not silently rewrite historical models.

4

Monitor model operations

Track extraction errors, schema validation, source-link quality, reviews, and costs.

A formatted AI response can still be wrong.

5

Manage contact requests

Use the planned private Inbox for requests and notification status.

A stored request and a delivered email are separate outcomes.

6

Maintain and audit

Review access, log privileged changes, test recovery, and apply retention policies.

Retention and deletion must consider derived outputs too.

Shared definitions

A context template defines a decision family, criteria, units, assessment scales, and required questions.

Individual circumstances

Each user retains their own goals, constraints, preferences, evidence, and unresolved uncertainties.

Example: product launch

Two topics can share demand evidence, readiness, cost, and learning value as criteria. One user may prioritize learning; another may prioritize speed. Their recommendations can legitimately differ.

Origin and review are separate

Origin: user statement, AI proposal, advisor assessment, or external source. Review: draft, user-confirmed, advisor-reviewed, or disputed.

Scenarios are not actual changes

“What if our budget fell?” should create a scenario. “Our budget has fallen” may propose a current-model update. Keep both source references and history.

Questions and boundaries

Important distinctions before using a decision-support workflow.

Does the AI make the final decision?

No. The proposed process structures options, sources, and trade-offs. Users retain judgment and can seek suitable professional advice for consequential decisions.

Can advisors see every conversation?

Not under this proposed policy. Access is limited by assignments and explicit sharing scope. Verify the implemented policy before entering confidential information.

Does similarity authorize a merge?

No. Similarity can suggest candidates, but permission and context compatibility must be checked. Synthesis remains separate from source models.

What if the model gets something wrong?

Flag the item, inspect the source, and propose a correction. Save a new reviewed version rather than silently rewriting history.

What does a score of 75 out of 100 mean?

Only what the explicitly defined scoring method says. It is not a 75% probability of success. Show scales, weights, criteria, and assumptions.

Does this guide connect to my account or Supabase?

No. This is a static guide with client-side tabs. It does not sign in users, change roles, save models, or send emails.

Are all features described here available now?

No. The guide includes planned workflows. Implementation, permission testing, and operational validation are required before they can be offered.
