One conversation. One topic model. A traceable next step.

The conversation is the discussion. The topic model is a structured interpretation that people can inspect, correct, and review. A synthesis is a separate model derived from selected, authorized sources.

What a topic model contains

Decision question, goals, constraints, options, criteria, assumptions, evidence references, information gaps, review status, and version history.

What it does not establish

An AI-generated statement is not a verified fact. A user report is not independent evidence. A preference score is not an outcome probability.

For users

Turn your conversation into a clear decision model

Explore one problem at a time. Keep your goals, options, evidence, and uncertainties visible—and correct the model when it gets your meaning wrong.

1✎

Define your topic

Describe the decision you face, your situation, and what a useful outcome would look like.

Example: Should we launch now or run a paid pilot?

2?

Clarify your needs

Answer focused questions about goals, constraints, priorities, time horizon, and missing context.

Confirm currency, dates, and what matters most.

3≡

Explore possible actions

Review suggested options and criteria. Add your own, revise suggestions, or reject options that do not fit.

An AI suggestion is not automatically your preference.

4◇

Review the topic model

Inspect the draft goals, options, assumptions, and evidence links. Correct omissions and unsupported statements.

User-reported information is not independently verified.

5⇄

Compare and seek review

Examine trade-offs and, when available, request input from an assigned advisor.

A preference score is not a probability of success.

6↻

Refine and choose a next step

Test hypothetical scenarios, review feedback, and save an updated model version.

Keep scenarios separate from your current situation.

Before you continue

  • Use a separate topic for an unrelated decision.
  • Confirm important model items before relying on them.
  • Do not enter passwords or unnecessary sensitive details.
  • Review the sharing scope before allowing cross-topic synthesis.

For advisors

Compare cases without erasing individual context

Review only authorized topics. Help users examine reasoning, identify gaps, and create traceable synthesis models when sharing permissions allow.

1✎

Select authorized topics

Choose the topics and model versions available within your assigned access.

Role membership alone should not expose every user.

2?

Review source and status

Inspect source-message references, evidence, unresolved questions, and user-confirmed items.

Separate AI drafts, user reports, and advisor assessments.

3≡

Check comparability

Confirm that the decision context, criteria definitions, units, time horizons, and scales are compatible.

Similar words do not establish equivalent circumstances.

4◇

Compare side by side

Look for shared patterns, conflicting assumptions, different constraints, and information gaps.

Do not average incompatible scores or hide disagreement.

5⇄

Create a separate synthesis

Select reusable elements and retain source-topic, version, and item references. Record why each item was included.

Do not overwrite the original users’ models.

6↻

Review and return feedback

Explain limitations, publish within the approved audience, and invite corrections or new versions.

Sharing a synthesis must not reveal unauthorized source details.

Before you continue

  • Check sharing permissions before selecting source models.
  • Keep an explanation for criterion mappings and exclusions.
  • Record uncertainty rather than inventing certainty.
  • Avoid implying that a group finding applies to every individual.

For administrators

Manage access, model quality, and operational safeguards

Keep platform administration separate from participant eligibility and advisor assignments. Make permissions, retention, and notification behavior explicit.

1✎

Manage identities and roles

Designate administrator accounts securely. Keep ordinary registration separate from administrator privileges.

A profile field or hidden menu is not an access-control rule.

2?

Configure access scopes

Manage participant eligibility, advisor assignments, and cross-user sharing rules. Enforce them on the backend.

Do not infer permission from similarity or account login alone.

3≡

Define context templates

Maintain decision families, criterion definitions, units, assessment scales, and template versions.

Template changes must not silently rewrite old models.

4◇

Monitor model operations

Track extraction failures, validation errors, source-link quality, review status, and usage costs.

AI output needs validation and human review.

5⇄

Manage contact requests

In the planned private Inbox, review requests and monitor pending or failed email notifications.

Website Inbox records and email delivery are separate outcomes.

6↻

Maintain and audit

Apply retention policies, test recovery, review privileged access, and record administrative changes.

Retention and deletion must account for derived models too.

Before you continue

  • Do not expose service-role or email-provider secrets in browser code.
  • Test permissions with separate user, advisor, and admin accounts.
  • Use spam protection and rate limits for public submissions.
  • Do not present a feature as available until it is implemented and tested.

Example: product-launch decisions

User A wants to learn before committing; User B prioritizes time to market. Both topics may share criteria such as demand evidence, readiness, cost, and learning value. Their criterion weights and recommendations can still differ.

A shared finding might be “Both cases lack willingness-to-pay evidence”—not “Both users should make the same decision.”

Origin

Who introduced this item?

User statement · AI proposal · advisor assessment · external source. These are different origins and should not be silently exchanged.

Review

Who checked its interpretation?

Draft · user-confirmed · advisor-reviewed · disputed. Review does not automatically verify a factual claim or its applicability.

Version 1→New message or evidence→Proposed change→Review→Version 2

“Our budget is now $12,000” may update the current model. “What if our budget fell to $12,000?” should create a scenario—not silently replace the current budget.

Questions and important boundaries

Does the AI make the final decision?

No. The proposed process helps structure options, sources, and trade-offs. The user retains responsibility for choosing what to do, and can seek appropriate professional advice for consequential decisions.

Can advisors see every user’s conversations?

Not under this proposed policy. Advisor access should be limited by assignment and explicit sharing rules. Confirm the implemented policy before entering confidential material.

Does similarity allow two topics to be merged?

No. Similarity can suggest candidates. Permission, context compatibility, source review, and advisor judgment must come first. A synthesis remains separate from its sources.

What if the model gets something wrong?

Flag the item, inspect its source, and propose a correction. Save a new reviewed version rather than silently rewriting the historical record.

What does a score of 75 out of 100 mean?

Only what the defined scoring method says it means. A preference score is not a 75% chance of success. Show criteria, scales, weights, and assumptions before interpreting a score.

Does this guide store information or change access?

No. This is a static explanatory page with native expandable questions. It does not connect to Supabase, perform sign-in, create model records, assign permissions, or send emails.
